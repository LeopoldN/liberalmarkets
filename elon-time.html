Elon Musk’s missed promises at Tesla and SpaceX

Fifteen years of public claims that turned out wrong, from full self-driving “next year” to people on Mars. Most are missed forecasts rather than proven lies. Where a court or regulator ruled, the entry says so.

By Nicholas Leopold 26 October 2026 15 min read

This is a dated catalog of public claims Elon Musk made about Tesla and SpaceX that turned out wrong, from 2011 to October 2026. Each entry gives when he said it, what he said (paraphrased unless quoted), the deadline he set, and what actually happened.

The entries fall into a few kinds, marked on each one:

  • Missed: a forecast or deadline that passed without the thing happening.
  • Late: the thing eventually happened, but years after the date he gave, or in a much smaller form.
  • False: a statement of fact that was untrue when made, or later found misleading by a court or regulator. Entries marked Misleading describe a presentation that left out a key fact, as reported by major outlets. Entries marked Reversed show a stated commitment later abandoned.
  • Pending: claims still open as of the date above, such as Mars in 2029 or Optimus volume.

Most entries are missed forecasts, not proven lies. A US court dismissed one investor suit over the self-driving claims as corporate puffery, while California’s DMV found Tesla’s Autopilot marketing misleading. Where a court or regulator ruled, the entry says so. Musk himself has called his timelines optimistic, and himself “the boy who cried FSD.”
